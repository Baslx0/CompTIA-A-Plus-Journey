# Study Method

I am using a deliberately simple workflow for CompTIA A+:

**Watch → Ask → Test → Find gaps → Practice → Continue**

## 1. Watch

I start with the relevant **Professor Messer** lesson.

I do not try to copy every sentence into notes. I focus on understanding the topic and recognizing anything that does not fully make sense.

## 2. Ask

If a concept is unclear, I stop and ask ChatGPT about that specific point.

The goal is not to replace the course with another lecture. I use it to get a different explanation, example, comparison, or troubleshooting context until the concept makes sense.

## 3. Test

After finishing a topic or objective, I use **ExamCompass** as an independent knowledge check.

I take the quiz without trying to memorize the question bank.

## 4. Find gaps

Wrong answers are useful because they reveal weak areas.

Instead of memorizing the exact answer, I go back to the concept behind the mistake.

## 5. Practice

When needed, I use additional practice such as:

- troubleshooting scenarios;
- PBQ-style questions;
- multiple-select questions;
- matching;
- command questions;
- short active-recall checks.

The point is to prove that I understand the idea in a different form.

## 6. Continue

Once the concept is clear and any important gap is resolved, I move forward.

I avoid repeatedly testing topics that I have already demonstrated I understand.

## The role of the exam objectives

The official CompTIA objectives are my **coverage roadmap**, not the ceiling of understanding.

If I need extra context to understand how a technology actually works, I learn it. At the same time, I avoid unnecessary deep dives that do not help the objective make sense.

## Note-taking rule

I do not want my notes to be a list of unexplained terms, but I also do not want to rewrite a textbook.

The target is:

> simple enough to review quickly, complete enough to understand the picture.

That means a useful note explains what a concept is, what it does, and any important distinction that helped me understand it.
