## Wi-Fi bands

### 2.4 GHz

Generally provides better range and wall penetration, but has fewer non-overlapping channels and usually more congestion/interference.

### 5 GHz

Provides more channel capacity than 2.4 GHz and is commonly used when higher performance and lower congestion are preferred over maximum range.

### 6 GHz

Provides additional spectrum and more room for non-overlapping channels, with less legacy-device congestion.

## Channel width

A wider channel can provide more throughput, but it also occupies more spectrum and can increase overlap/interference risk.

## Other wireless technologies

- **802.11:** IEEE standards used for WLAN communication.
- **Bluetooth:** short-range connectivity commonly used for PAN devices and peripherals.
- **NFC:** very short-range communication, such as contactless interactions.
- **RFID:** commonly used for identification and tracking.

## Key takeaway

Wireless performance is a trade-off between frequency, range, available spectrum, channel width, and interference.
